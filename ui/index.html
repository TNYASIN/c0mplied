Clear evidence. Careful language. Human review.

Clinical research should make sense to everyone it affects.

HerEvidence checks public clinical trial records for reporting gaps related to sex and participation. It shows what the record supports, what it does not, and where a person should look next.

Explore checked studiesAsk about the evidence

A missing item means “not identified in the registry information checked.” It never means a study was automatically biased, discriminatory, or unsafe.

New to clinical research? Start with these terms
Clinical trial
A research study involving people.
Registry record
A public summary of a study. It may not contain everything found in papers or the full protocol.
Reporting gap
Information was not identified in the public fields checked. It is a question to investigate, not a judgment.

How evidence is verified

The source stays in charge.

  1. 1
    Read structured records

    Enrollment counts come directly from registry fields.

  2. 2
    Require evidence

    Text findings keep the original excerpt and field path.

  3. 3
    Withhold uncertain work

    Unverified and quarantined findings are not presented as trusted research.

How to use HerEvidence

Three simple steps

You do not need medical or research training. Start anywhere and use the plain-language explanations.

  1. 1
    Choose the evidence

    Pick verified real records or clearly labelled demo data.

    Go to Explore
  2. 2
    Select a study

    Search by study ID, title, or health condition, then open its findings.

    See an explanation
  3. 3
    Ask a question

    Use a suggested question or type your own. Every factual answer says which records were checked.

    Ask HerEvidence
01

Why reporting matters

Clear reporting helps people understand who participated and what can—and cannot—be learned from a registry record.

02

What we investigate

HerEvidence checks enrollment by sex, sex-specific outcome reporting, and selected eligibility wording.

03

What we do not claim

A registry gap is not proof that an analysis was never done. Publications and full protocols may contain more detail.
